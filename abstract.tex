The strategic regions of Banten, DKI Jakarta, and West Java constitute the core national economic activity in Indonesia, which underwent significant structural variations during the 2020--2024 period as a consequence of the global pandemic and the subsequent recovery phase. Traditional multivariate approaches often encounter limitations in capturing the simultaneous interactions between spatial units, social indicators, and temporal shifts within a unified framework. This research addresses this gap by applying the \f{STATIS} (\f{Structuration des Tableaux à Trois Indices de la Statistique}) method, a robust three-way data analysis framework, to synthesize a stable compromise structure that represents the common development patterns across 41 regencies and cities. The objective is to evaluate the longitudinal stability of 11 strategic development indicators and map the spatio-temporal trajectories to differentiate regional resilience. The methodology involves weighted $Z$-score standardization using demographic weights, inter-structure evaluation through $RV$ coefficients, and the construction of a compromise representation ($\mathbf{W}_c$) via optimal spectral weighting. A Monte Carlo permutation test ($B=1000$) is employed to validate the significance of the discovered structure, as illustrated in the schematic framework in Chapter 3.

Analysis of the inter-structure reveals an exceptionally high degree of consistency in the spatial configurations across the five-year period, with all $RV$ coefficients exceeding $0.90$ ($p < 0.05$). This demonstrates that while the absolute magnitudes of development indicators fluctuated, the underlying relative hierarchy and structural relationships between regions, between indicators, and across years remained remarkably persistent, as summarized in Table~\ref{tab:rv_summary_ext}. The resulting compromise structure accounts for $71.88\%$ of the total variance through two principal dimensions (Figure~\ref{fig:biplot_ext}). Dimension~1 represents the primary axis of ``Human Development and Economic Advancement,'' showing strong positive correlations with GRDP, HDI, and Mean Years of Schooling, alongside strong negative correlations with Poverty Rate. Dimension~2 captures ``Labor Market Pressure,'' primarily driven by the Open Unemployment Rate (loading $+0.86$) and the Labor Force Participation Rate (loading $-0.49$).

The projection of yearly data into the compromise space through a \f{Dynamic Biplot} reveals three distinct regional trajectory patterns (Figure~\ref{fig:traj_ext}). Service-based metropolitan centers, most notably Kota Tangerang Selatan and all DKI Jakarta cities, exhibited a characteristic V-shape recovery pattern, where an initial decline in 2020--2021 was followed by a robust structural rebound; Kota Tangerang Selatan recorded the largest PC1 increase ($+0.85$ points) over the 2020--2024 period. In contrast, regencies in the southern parts of Banten and West Java, including Kabupaten Pangandaran ($-1.37$ points) and Garut ($-0.61$ points), displayed stagnant or declining trajectories, indicating that regional development gaps widened rather than narrowed. Several buffer-zone cities such as Kota Sukabumi demonstrated progressive growth patterns, successfully advancing to a higher cluster in 2023.

Advanced clustering using the K-Means algorithm ($k=3$) on the compromise coordinates successfully partitioned 41 regions into three groups: Cluster~1 ``Underdeveloped'' (12 regions, predominantly southern Banten and inland southern West Java), Cluster~2 ``Developing'' (16 regions, industrial corridors and buffer zones), and Cluster~3 ``Advanced'' (13 metropolitan regions). The temporal transition matrix confirmed perfect stability in 2020--2021, followed by the largest restructuring in 2023--2024, where 14 regions moved from Cluster~2 to Cluster~1 and 4 regions from Cluster~3 to Cluster~2, while ten core metropolitan regions remained in Cluster~3 throughout the entire period. External validation against normative UNDP HDI categories confirmed a high degree of structural alignment, with 11 of 13 Cluster~3 members carrying the ``Very High'' HDI classification. These findings indicate that regional disparities in the Banten--DKI Jakarta--West Java area are driven by deep-seated structural factors, necessitating targeted policy interventions for underdeveloped regions rather than uniform development strategies. In conclusion, the STATIS method provides a comprehensive longitudinal perspective that outperforms traditional static approaches in monitoring regional dynamics. The structural stability of the compromise representation demonstrates that the core drivers of regional inequality remained persistent despite the global pandemic. A Monte Carlo permutation test ($B = 1000$) validated that the discovered structure is statistically significant ($p < 0.05$) and not attributable to random fluctuation. The interactive dashboard application developed using the Dash/Plotly framework provides a dynamic visual exploration tool for researchers and policymakers to independently monitor regional development trajectories and design evidence-based strategies for equitable regional development.
