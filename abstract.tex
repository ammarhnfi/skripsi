The strategic regions of Banten, DKI Jakarta, and West Java constitute the core national economic agglomeration in Indonesia, which underwent significant structural variations during the 2020--2024 period as a consequence of the global pandemic and the subsequent recovery phase. Traditional multivariate approaches often encounter limitations in capturing the simultaneous interactions between spatial units, social indicators, and temporal shifts within a unified framework. This research addresses this gap by applying the \f{STATIS} (\f{Structuration des Tableaux à Trois Indices de la Statistique}) method, a robust three-way data analysis framework, to synthesize a stable compromise structure that represents the common development patterns across 41 regencies and cities. The objective is to evaluate the longitudinal stability of 11 strategic development indicators and map the spatio-temporal trajectories to differentiate regional resilience. The methodology involves weighted $Z$-score standardization using demographic weights, inter-structure evaluation through $RV$ coefficients, and the construction of a compromise representation ($\mathbf{W}_c$) via optimal spectral weighting. A Monte Carlo permutation test ($B=1000$) is employed to validate the significance of the discovered structure, as illustrated in the schematic framework in Chapter 3.

Analysis of the inter-structure reveals an exceptionally high degree of consistency in the spatial configurations across the five-year period, with all $RV$ coefficients exceeding $0.90$ ($p < 0.001$). This suggests that while absolute economic levels experienced fluctuations, the underlying relative hierarchy and structural relationships between regencies remained remarkably persistent, as summarized in Table \ref{tab:rv_summary_ext}. The resulting compromise structure accounts for over $85\%$ of the total variance through two principal dimensions (Figure \ref{fig:biplot_ext}). Dimension 1 represents the primary axis of "Socio-Economic Capacity," showing strong positive correlations with GRDP, HDI, and Mean Years of Schooling. Conversely, Dimension 2 captures "Social and Health Resilience," primarily driven by variations in Life Expectancy and food security indicators.

The projection of yearly data into the compromise space through a \f{Dynamic Biplot} reveals three distinct regional trajectories (Figure \ref{fig:traj_ext}). Metropolitan centers, such as Jakarta Pusat and Bandung, exhibited a characteristic V-shape recovery pattern, where an initial contraction in 2020--2021 was followed by a robust structural rebound by 2024. In contrast, regencies in the southern parts of Banten and West Java, including Lebak and Garut, showed stagnant trajectories, indicating limited structural response to recovery stimuli. Furthermore, several buffer zones demonstrated progressive growth patterns, reflecting a steady enhancement of their socio-economic profiles.

Advanced clustering using the K-Means algorithm ($k=3$) on the compromise coordinates successfully partitioned the regions into "Advanced," "Developing," and "Underdeveloped" clusters. External validation against normative UNDP HDI categories confirmed a high degree of structural alignment, particularly for the Advanced cluster which consistently comprised regions with "Very High" human development. These findings imply that regional disparities in Western Java are driven by deep-seated structural factors that are relatively invariant to short-term shocks, necessitating targeted policy interventions rather than uniform regional strategies.

In conclusion, the STATIS method provides a comprehensive longitudinal perspective that outperforms traditional static approaches in monitoring regional dynamics. The structural stability of the compromise representation demonstrates that the core drivers of regional inequality remained persistent despite the global pandemic. The developed interactive system serves as a sophisticated analytical tool for policymakers to monitor resilience and design data-driven strategies for regional development equity.
