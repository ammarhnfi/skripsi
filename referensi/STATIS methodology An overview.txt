Abstract. The STATIS (Structuration des Tableaux a Trois Indices de la Statistique) methodology is a data analysis technique 
which computes Euclidean distances between studies of the same observations obtained in k diﬀerent circumstances. The 
Escouﬁer operators are used to obtain a geometrical representation for the studies in a series. In this study we present 
developments in the STATIS methodology, namely how to make inferences when for each treatment of a model we have a series of 
studies.
Introduction
The Analyse Conjointe de Tableaux (STATIS method Structuration des Tableaux a Trois Indices de la Statistique) is 
an exploratory technique of multivariate data analysis based on linear algebra and especially on Euclidean vector 
spaces. This method was introduced by [3], and developed by several authors, in particular by [4] and [5]. STATIS 
can be used to analyze data in a variety of ﬁelds, including agriculture, environmental science, sociology, and 
psychology. It is a powerful tool for exploring complex data structures and identifying relationships between 
multiple variables, but it is important to be cautious about the results and to interpret them in the context of the data 
and the assumptions of the methodology. The central idea of this methodology is to compare conﬁgurations of the 
same individuals or variables in diﬀerent circumstances or moments in time. Therefore, this method allows us 
not only to identify the moments in time in which the most signiﬁcant changes occurred, but also to rank variables 
and individuals according to their contributions to the total changes. We are also able to draw the trajectory of 
each individual or variable around its compromise (average) position. In the STATIS methodology we study series 
of studies. The studies are matrix triplets
(Xi, Dpi , Dn), where the ﬁrst matrix is a data matrix where columns contains the values of the considered variables in

Structure families of series of studies
Up to now we have considered a single series of studies. Now we assume having for each treatment of a model such a
series. Our goal will be to study the action of the factors in that model on the structure vectors. Two types of analysis
are considered:
•
Transversal Analysis on the homologous components of the structure vectors. Usually only the ﬁrst such vectors
are considered;
•
Longitudinal Analysis on linear applications on the estimated structure vectors. This type is now adequate when
these is a progressive in the studies of the series. For instance if they were carried out in successive times. As
stated above these analysis are usually carried out for the ﬁrst structure vectors since, many times, they are the
sole ones for which we have estimators with suﬃcient precisions.
Several applications are presented has led to other useful approaches to data analysis ([1]; [7]). [1] consider the
validation of these model studied of local Portuguese elections of years (1985, 1989, 1993, 1997 and 2001). In this
study, four districts stretching from North to South along the coast and four others along the border with Spain were
selected. For each of these districts, two townships were selected. One of them contained the district capitalcity. Thus
we had good coverage of the territory, being able to apply the proposed model to the 16 series of studies. That model
was based on a new dimension for modelling the inter-structure of a series of studies in the ACT method.
Final remarks
STATIS methodology is used to identify patterns or structures in the data and to explore the relationships between
multiple variables. Although to have a solid algebraic formulation, the STATIS methodology only gives empirical
conclusions. The development of models has allowed for the possibility of making inference about the relationships
between variables and to test hypotheses about the underlying structures in the data. This provides a deeper under-
standing of the data and allows for the development of predictions and insights about the population and can help
researchers to make more informed decisions and to further advance their understanding of the relationships between
the variables.
